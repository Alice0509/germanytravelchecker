Berlin trouble guide

Berlin checks for small travel troubles.

Berlin is international and flexible in many ways, but German Sunday and public holiday rules still matter. Use this guide to avoid common trouble with water, pharmacies, groceries, cash, Pfand and trip timing.

Federal state

Berlin is both a city and a federal state, so holiday checking is simpler than in some other places. If your trip is in Berlin, use the Berlin state calendar for public holidays and school holidays.

Public holidays

Regular shops and supermarkets are usually closed on public holidays in Germany. Public holidays can vary by federal state, so check your exact Berlin date before relying on a specific shop or service.

Sunday closures

Many regular shops and supermarkets are usually closed on Sundays. Berlin may feel more flexible than smaller cities, but do not assume a normal supermarket will be open. Travelers often check major stations, airports, gas stations, kiosks, cafés, bakeries, restaurants or hotels for essentials.

Groceries and essentials

For water, snacks or basic groceries, Berlin travelers often check transport hubs, gas stations, kiosks, cafés, bakeries, restaurants or hotel reception first. The city has many options, but exact opening hours still vary.

Pharmacies and emergency help

Regular pharmacies may be closed on Sundays and public holidays. Emergency pharmacies change by date and location, so check official Notdienst-Apotheke services, Google Maps or pharmacy websites.

For medical emergencies call 112. For police emergencies call 110. For non-emergency medical on-call help outside regular office hours, travelers in Germany can check 116117.

Small Germany troubles to know

  • Public toilets may cost money, especially around stations or busy areas. Keep a few coins or small cash handy.
  • Some kiosks, bakeries or small places may not accept every card, so a payment backup can save stress.
  • Pfand is a bottle deposit. The price may look higher, but you can usually get the deposit back when returning the bottle.
  • If you want still water, look for stilles Wasser, ohne Kohlensäure or naturell.
  • If the bottle says Sprudel, Classic or mit Kohlensäure, it is usually sparkling.
