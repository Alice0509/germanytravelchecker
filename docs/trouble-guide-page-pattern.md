# Trouble Guide Page Pattern

## Purpose

Trouble guide pages help English-speaking visitors quickly understand sudden Germany travel problems.

They are not generic travel articles.

They should explain what is happening, what the German words mean, what to do next and where to verify current details.

## Core Structure

Every trouble guide should follow this pattern:

1. Problem
2. Meaning
3. What to do
4. Verify

This keeps pages useful for travelers who may be tired, carrying luggage, holding a child’s hand or trying to make a connection.

## Content Blocks

Each guide should include:

- plain-English title
- short human lead paragraph
- German words to recognize
- meaning in plain English
- practical next steps
- verification sources
- trust boundary or safety note

## Good Examples

Train disruption guide:

- Problem: You see `SEV` at a station.
- Meaning: Part of the rail route may be replaced by a bus.
- What to do: Look for SEV, Ersatzverkehr or Bus signs and allow extra time.
- Verify: DB Navigator, local transport app, station signs or staff.

Water label guide:

- Problem: You want still water but the bottle labels are confusing.
- Meaning: German labels distinguish still, lightly sparkling and sparkling water.
- What to do: Look for stilles Wasser, ohne Kohlensäure or naturell.
- Verify: Bottle label.

## Trust Rules

Trouble guides must not claim:

- live train disruption data
- exact shop opening hours
- exact emergency pharmacy duty schedules
- medical diagnosis
- guaranteed availability of stations, kiosks, toilets, vending machines or pharmacies

They should point users to official or live sources when details can change.

## Future Use

This pattern should support:

- static trouble guide pages
- Trouble Finder results
- city guide cards
- future Arrival Trouble Check
- future AI question box

The AI layer, if added later, should use the same structured trouble topics instead of inventing answers.
